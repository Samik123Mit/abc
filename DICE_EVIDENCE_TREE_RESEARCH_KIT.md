# DICE Evidence Tree and Primary Research Kit

## Purpose

This document is the research operating system for the Meesho DICE BPC case.

The goal is not to prove the preferred idea.

The goal is to identify:
- where the creator-led BPC value chain leaks
- which hypothesis has the strongest evidence
- which bottleneck has the highest NMV and retention leverage
- which single wedge should be piloted in the first 30 days

Strategic direction is locked:

`Creator x SKU x Audience Decision Engine`

Diagnosis and wedge remain provisional.

Do not build the final PPT until the research gate is complete.

---

## 1. Evidence Tree

### Root question

`Where does value leak between creator discovery and BPC repeat purchase?`

Context question:

`Why is BPC under-indexed in Meesho creator commerce relative to category demand?`

### Funnel to map

`Brand -> creator selection -> product trial -> content creation -> content distribution -> Meesho click -> PDP consideration -> purchase -> return -> repeat -> creator payout`

H1-H5 are possible leakage points in this funnel. They are not mutually exclusive.

### Branches

#### Creator side

Question:
- Why do creators not consistently promote BPC?

Hypotheses:
- H1: They cannot identify products that fit their audience.
- H2: Trial cost and effort discourage experimentation.
- H3: BPC content is harder to make credibly.
- H5: Expected earnings do not justify time, risk, and product cost.

Evidence to collect:
- recent products promoted
- products considered but rejected
- how products were discovered
- self-funded versus gifted trials
- time spent per content asset
- expected versus realized earnings
- audience response and conversion

#### User side

Question:
- Why do users who discover beauty content fail to purchase?

Hypothesis:
- H4: Users lack enough proof on authenticity, suitability, or expected result.

Evidence to collect:
- last online beauty purchase
- discovery source
- information checked before purchase
- reasons for delaying or abandoning
- creator similarity signals used
- review and return behavior
- platform comparison

#### Brand or seller side

Question:
- Why do brands not scale influencer-led BPC more aggressively?

Relevant hypotheses:
- H1: They cannot identify creator-SKU fit.
- H4: Creator content does not transfer enough proof to commerce.
- H5: Attribution and ROI are not strong enough.

Evidence to collect:
- creator selection method
- campaign objective
- sampling budget
- preferred creator size and profile
- attribution setup
- repeat purchase visibility
- return and counterfeit concerns
- budget re-spend decision

## 2. Hypothesis Register

| ID | Hypothesis | Falsifier | Evidence needed | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Creators and brands struggle with creator-SKU-audience fit | Most respondents already have an easy, reliable matching method | Interviews, product-flow audit, competitor scan | Open |
| H2 | Trial cost and effort suppress BPC creator activation | Creators receive enough relevant samples or cost has no effect on behavior | Creator interviews, price/commission model, sampling-flow audit | Open |
| H3 | BPC requires more proof-rich content than generic affiliate categories | Creators report comparable effort and content requirements across categories | Creator interviews, content audit, expert input | Open |
| H4 | Discovery does not reliably become purchase because shoppers lack decision proof | Users report that proof is not important or already sufficient | User interviews/survey, PDP audit, abandonment reasons | Open |
| H5 | Weak economics dominate creator category choice | Economics clearly ranks above fit, content effort, and proof in behavior | Creator interviews, payout math, time-cost model | Open |

### Desk-research scoreboard

This is a prior based on the case brief and public desk research only.

It is not a finding from interviews or a representative survey.

Scoring:
- observed strength: 0 to 5
- 0 means no current support
- 5 means strong current support
- primary validation: 0 means not collected yet

| Hypothesis | What would support it? | What would weaken or kill it? | Current observed strength | Primary validation | Current read |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| H1: Creator-SKU mismatch | Creators repeatedly report uncertainty about which SKUs fit their audience; brands lack reliable creator-SKU selection; mismatch appears in failed content or low conversion | Creators confidently choose relevant SKUs and mismatch rarely causes failure | 3/5 | 0/5 | High-priority hypothesis, not a finding |
| H2: Sampling friction | Creators want to test products but cannot access, afford, or receive relevant samples | Most creators already obtain relevant products easily and access does not affect behavior | 3/5 | 0/5 | Plausible activation barrier |
| H3: Content difficulty | BPC creators struggle to demonstrate shade, texture, efficacy, or suitability compared with non-BPC creators | Creators report comparable effort and content is already standardized | 4/5 | 0/5 | Strong case-supported hypothesis |
| H4: User proof gap | Users hesitate because creator content or PDPs do not answer suitability, authenticity, or expected-result questions | Users already receive sufficient proof and proof does not affect decisions | 4/5 | 0/5 | Strong case-supported hypothesis |
| H5: Creator economics | Earnings, time, trial cost, or attribution are major reasons creators stop promoting BPC | Economics are attractive and inactivity has another dominant cause | 2/5 | 0/5 | Plausible but currently weaker |
| N1: Generic creator-commerce friction | The same leakage appears in beauty and non-beauty, meaning the problem is not BPC-specific | Non-BPC creators move smoothly while BPC has distinct leakage | 2/5 | 0/5 | Control hypothesis |

### Interpretation rule

Do not select the highest desk score as the winner.

The desk score only determines interview order:
1. H1, H3, and H4 first
2. H2 and H5 next
3. N1 as a control comparison

The final wedge should be chosen only after primary validation and funnel-impact scoring.

## 3. Research Rules

- Ask about recent behavior, not opinions about our proposed solution.
- Ask for concrete examples, dates, products, and tradeoffs.
- Separate reported behavior from interpretation.
- Record verbatim phrases where permission is given.
- Never turn a small sample into a market-wide percentage.
- Label public media claims as secondary evidence.
- Label our own calculations as estimates or hypotheses.
- Do not claim a Meesho-specific bottleneck without Meesho-specific evidence.
- Do not ask respondents to validate “Creator x SKU x Audience Decision Engine.”

## 4. Creator Interview Kit

Target:
- 7 to 10 Meesho beauty and non-beauty creators, as requested in the case brief
- Include activated creators and power creators
- Include at least two creators from smaller cities or vernacular audiences if possible

Opening:

`We are students researching creator-led beauty commerce for a case competition. We are not selling a product and are not asking you to endorse an idea. We want to understand what you actually do when deciding what to promote.`

### Recent behavior

1. Think about the last three products you promoted. What were they?
2. How did you discover each product?
3. What made you choose each product over other products?
4. What products did you consider but decide not to promote?
5. What was the biggest reason for rejecting them?

### BPC-specific behavior

6. How many BPC products have you considered or promoted in the last three months?
7. Which BPC categories are easiest to create content for?
8. Which BPC categories are hardest to recommend credibly?
9. Have you personally used a product before recommending it?
10. What information do you need before you feel comfortable recommending a beauty product?

### Trial and sampling

11. Have you ever paid for a product before creating content about it?
12. How do product cost, delivery time, and effort affect your decision to test a product?
13. What would make you test a product you have not used before?
14. Have you received samples or products through a creator program? What worked and what did not?

### Audience fit

15. How do you decide whether a product suits your audience?
16. Do followers ask about skin type, shade, undertone, hair type, ingredients, or results?
17. Can you recall a product that performed well because it fit your audience unusually well?
18. Can you recall a product that performed poorly despite good views?

### Content effort

19. How long does a typical BPC content asset take from product receipt to posting?
20. What makes a BPC recommendation believable rather than promotional?
21. Which details are difficult to explain in a short video?
22. What information do you wish brands or platforms provided before you created content?

### Economics and attribution

23. What matters more when choosing a product: commission, conversion probability, product fit, audience interest, or content effort?
24. How do you know whether a post actually worked?
25. What frustrates you about links, attribution, payouts, returns, or reporting?
26. What would make you promote a category more consistently?

### Neutral closing

27. If you could change one part of the current creator-commerce workflow, what would you change?
28. What have I not asked that matters to your decision?

Do not ask:
- `Would you use our matching engine?`
- `Would Beauty Proof solve this?`
- `Would you like free samples?`

Those questions lead the respondent.

## 5. User Interview and Survey Kit

Target:
- 10 to 20 qualitative user conversations if available
- larger lightweight survey only if distribution is realistic
- include Meesho users and non-Meesho online beauty shoppers
- include Tier 2/3 and metro respondents where possible

Opening:

`We are studying how people buy beauty products online. Please answer based on your last real purchase, not what you think you might do.`

### Last-purchase interview

1. Tell me about the last beauty or personal-care product you bought online.
2. How did you first discover it?
3. Which platforms or creators did you check?
4. What information did you look for before purchasing?
5. What almost stopped you from buying?
6. Did you compare another product or platform?
7. Did you check whether the creator had similar skin, hair, shade, or use case?
8. What made the product feel authentic or safe?
9. What information was missing?
10. Did you return or regret a beauty product in the last six months? Why?

### Ranking prompt

Ask the user to rank the top three purchase concerns:
- authenticity
- shade match
- skin or hair suitability
- ingredients
- texture or finish
- visible result
- reviews
- price and value
- creator similarity
- delivery or returns

### Behavior-based survey items

Use a five-point scale only if a survey is run:
- I compare beauty products across platforms before purchasing.
- I look for creators or reviewers with similar skin or hair characteristics.
- I need more information for beauty than for fashion.
- I have delayed a beauty purchase because I was unsure it would suit me.
- I have returned or regretted a beauty purchase because the product did not match expectations.
- Creator content helps me discover products.
- Reviews or proof help me decide whether to purchase.

Do not present the proposed solution before these questions.

## 6. Brand and Seller Interview Kit

Target:
- 3 to 5 BPC sellers, brands, or agency practitioners if accessible

Questions:

1. What are you optimizing for when working with creators?
2. How do you select creators for a SKU?
3. What makes a creator a good fit for a product?
4. How do you decide whether to send samples?
5. What percentage of creator content converts into measurable sales?
6. What makes attribution difficult?
7. Do you see meaningful differences by creator language, geography, price band, or audience?
8. What makes you repeat spend with a creator?
9. What causes you to stop a campaign?
10. What is the biggest problem with influencer-led BPC today?

Use one row per evidence item:

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence ID | C-001 / U-001 / B-001 / S-001 |
| Source type | Creator / User / Brand / Secondary / Product audit |
| Date | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Respondent or source | Anonymous code or URL |
| Exact observation | What was said or observed |
| Related hypothesis | H1-H5 |
| Supports or weakens | Supports / weakens / neutral |
| Frequency | Number of independent occurrences |
| Economic relevance | NMV / retention / both / unclear |
| Confidence | Low / medium / high |
| Action | Keep / modify / deprioritize / kill |

Allowed evidence tags:
- H1
- H2
- H3
- H4
- H5
- N1: generic creator-commerce friction
- NEW: unexpected issue not covered by the initial tree

Allowed polarity tags:
- Supports
- Neutral
- Contradicts

Do not force an observation into H1-H5 if it clearly indicates a new issue.

Evidence quality rules:
- one respondent is a signal, not a finding
- repeated behavior beats stated preference
- direct product-flow observation beats generic commentary
- contradictory evidence must be recorded, not discarded

## 9. Scoring Model

Score each hypothesis from 1 to 5.

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence strength | How direct and credible is the evidence? |
| Frequency | How often does the issue recur independently? |
| NMV leverage | Could fixing it materially change orders or conversion? |
| Retention leverage | Could fixing it materially improve creator activity? |
| Feasibility | Can Meesho test a response within 30 days? |
| Defensibility | Does the response create compounding advantage? |

Suggested weighted score:

`Priority = 25% evidence strength + 20% frequency + 20% NMV leverage + 15% retention leverage + 10% feasibility + 10% defensibility`

This weighting is a research framework, not a fact. Adjust it only if new evidence makes a different weighting more defensible.

For primary research, add:

`Observed strength = independent supporting observations / relevant respondents`

Do not report this as a population percentage. Report the denominator and sample type:

`6 of 10 interviewed creators mentioned trial cost as a major barrier.`

That is a sample finding, not a market-wide statistic.

## 10. Research Kill Criteria

### H1: Creator x SKU mismatch

Kill or materially modify H1 if:
- creators consistently report that product discovery is easy
- brands already have a reliable matching method
- mismatch does not affect content or conversion outcomes

### H2: Sampling friction

Kill or materially modify H2 if:
- creators already receive enough relevant products
- product cost and trial effort do not affect promotion behavior
- sampling does not change willingness to test

### H3: Content difficulty

Kill or materially modify H3 if:
- creators report BPC content is no harder than other categories
- structured proof adds effort without improving usefulness

### H4: User proof gap

Kill or materially modify H4 if:
- users say proof is not important
- users already have sufficient suitability and authenticity information
- proof does not influence purchase confidence in observed behavior

### H5: Weak economics

Elevate H5 if:
- creator economics dominates category choice
- expected earnings minus effort and trial cost is the main decision rule
- fit and proof are secondary in repeated behavior

### Strategic-direction kill criterion

Reconsider the full Creator x SKU x Audience Decision Engine if:
- there is no meaningful matching problem
- matching does not affect NMV or retention
- the required data cannot be accessed or approximated
- a simpler intervention explains and fixes the majority of the leakage

## 11. Research Gate Before PPT

Do not build the final deck until we have:

- creator interviews completed or transparently marked as pending
- user research completed or transparently marked as pending
- seller or brand input attempted
- Meesho creator and BPC product-flow audit
- competitor comparison
- evidence table populated
- H1-H5 scored
- one wedge selected
- impact equation tied to measurable funnel nodes
- contradictions and kill criteria documented

The final deck should be able to say:

`We investigated five failure points. Evidence showed [X] was the highest-leverage controllable bottleneck. Therefore, we selected [Y] as the 30-day wedge.`

It should not say:

`We liked Beauty Proof, so we built evidence around it.`
