# DICE Provisional Evidence Pack

## Scope

This is a provisional desk-research pack for the Meesho DICE BPC case.

It is not a substitute for primary interviews.

It separates:
- observed public evidence
- inference
- open research gaps
- the current wedge recommendation

No interviews, survey responses, or Meesho internal data are being fabricated.

Research status:
- strategic direction: locked
- diagnosis: provisional
- wedge: pending primary validation

Strategic direction:

`Creator x SKU x Audience Decision Engine`

---

## 1. Executive Finding

Public evidence suggests Meesho already has meaningful creator-commerce supply and traction:
- Meesho launched Creator Club in February 2025.
- Public coverage says content commerce reached more than `14.5M` users in its first year.
- Meesho reported a `3X` increase in content-commerce order volume during 2024.
- Public reports cite more than `21,000` creators or influencers.
- The program includes creator-seller collaboration, real-time performance analytics, faster payouts, and direct collaboration.
- Meesho's Video Finds and Live Shop formats connect content to in-app shopping.

Therefore, the first diagnosis should not be:

`Meesho lacks creators or content-commerce infrastructure.`

The stronger provisional diagnosis is:

`Meesho has creator-commerce distribution, but BPC may not yet have enough category-specific matching, proof structure, and conversion feedback to turn that distribution into scalable BPC NMV.`

This is an inference, not a proven internal fact.

## 4. What Public Evidence Rules Out

### Not enough to say “get more creators”

Meesho's public materials already describe a substantial creator/content-commerce base. A proposal that only acquires creators is unlikely to feel insightful.

### Not enough to say “increase commissions”

Commissions, payouts, and analytics are already visible program levers. A commission-only proposal is easy to copy and does not address BPC suitability or proof.

### Not enough to say “send free samples”

Sampling is useful, but it is a mechanism. The strategic question is:

`Which creator should receive which SKU for which audience, and what proof should be generated from that trial?`

### Not enough to say “add reviews”

The case requires a three-sided scalable model. A downstream review widget does not solve creator activation or brand-side matching by itself.

## 5. Provisional Diagnosis

### Observed system

`Creator -> Content -> Meesho discovery -> Product page -> Purchase`

### What appears to exist

- creator supply
- affiliate or commission mechanics
- in-app content commerce
- direct creator-seller collaboration
- analytics and faster payouts

### What is not publicly established

- BPC-specific creator-SKU matching
- audience-fit matching
- structured suitability metadata from creator content
- proof transfer from social content into BPC PDPs
- BPC-specific creator economics
- return-adjusted BPC affiliate performance

### Provisional bottleneck

`The likely missing capability is not content distribution; it is converting general creator distribution into category-specific, audience-relevant, measurable BPC proof.`

This is the current research hypothesis.

## 6. What We Need To Validate Directly

### H1: Creator x SKU mismatch

Validate:
- how creators currently discover BPC SKUs
- whether they choose based on audience fit or commission alone
- whether brands manually match creators
- whether creators can describe their audience in usable product attributes

Kill signal:
- creators already have a reliable, low-friction matching method

### H2: Sampling friction

Validate:
- how many BPC products creators self-fund
- whether trial cost changes promotion behavior
- whether a targeted sample changes willingness to post
- whether sampling without fit creates waste

Kill signal:
- sample access exists and is not a meaningful behavioral barrier

### H3: Content difficulty

Validate:
- time required to produce credible BPC content
- information creators need before recommending
- whether audience questions are about suitability or only price
- whether structured prompts improve content quality

Kill signal:
- BPC content is no harder than generic affiliate content

### H4: User proof gap

Validate:
- where users abandon from discovery to purchase
- which proof fields matter most
- whether creator similarity is used in real behavior
- whether authenticity, shade, skin, hair, and ingredient concerns drive delay

Kill signal:
- proof is not a material purchase decision factor

### H5: Weak economics

Validate:
- expected earnings by category
- time spent per BPC asset
- product cost and trial cost
- returns or failed conversions
- minimum expected earnings required to post consistently

Elevate signal:
- earnings minus effort and trial cost dominates every other choice

## 7. Recommended Primary Research Sample

Minimum credible first pass:
- 7 to 10 creators, split between beauty and non-beauty
- 10 to 20 users who bought beauty online recently
- 3 to 5 BPC sellers, brands, or agency practitioners
- manual audit of at least 30 BPC product pages
- manual audit of at least 10 creator-commerce journeys

Do not present these as completed until actually completed.

If access is limited, say:

`Primary research was initiated with [n] completed responses; findings are directional and not statistically representative.`

That is more credible than manufacturing a larger sample.

## 8. Interim Winner Recommendation

The strategic direction remains:

`Creator x SKU x Audience Decision Engine`

The first wedge is not fully locked.

However, the strongest current candidate is:

`Beauty Proof with targeted sample allocation`

Why it currently leads:
- H1, H3, and H4 can be addressed together.
- Sampling is used selectively rather than positioned as the product.
- The system produces structured data for future matching.
- It is testable without a full machine-learning build.

Fallback wedge decisions:
- If H2 dominates: Smart Sampling first.
- If H4 dominates: PDP Proof Layer first.
- If H5 dominates: Quality-linked Creator Economics first.
- If H1 dominates with adequate content: Match-first creator recommendation.

## 9. Pilot Design Before Internal Data Exists

Do not claim pilot uplift yet.

Use a controlled pilot design:

Treatment:
- matched creator-SKU pairs
- targeted sample or product access
- structured proof brief
- proof surfaced on a controlled Meesho surface

Comparison:
- similar creator-SKU pairs using the current workflow

Primary outcomes:
- incremental creator-led BPC NMV
- click-to-order conversion
- creator retention

Secondary outcomes:
- proof completeness
- return rate
- repeat purchase
- creator earnings per hour
- brand re-spend intent

Decision rule:
- scale only if the treatment improves NMV or conversion without unacceptable return or sample wastage

## 10. Deck-Ready Insight Statements

Use only after the underlying evidence is validated:

1. `Meesho already has creator-commerce distribution; the next unlock is category-specific productivity.`
2. `In BPC, creator value is not reach alone. It is the ability to reduce uncertainty for the right audience.`
3. `The common question across the marketplace is fit: is this creator right for this SKU and this audience?`
4. `Sampling is not the moat. The moat is the learning loop created by matched trials and measurable proof.`
5. `The 30-day wedge should be the highest-leverage controllable leakage point, not the most fashionable feature.`
